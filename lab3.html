1. Problem Scenario

Jasmine, a busy mom of two, wants to order fresh, healthy pastries for her kids’ school events but finds most bakery sites overwhelming. With cluttered menus and unclear options, she gets frustrated trying to find where to customize or pre-order treats. She's looking for a reliable local bakery that’s easy to navigate and lets her pre-schedule orders online.

2. Activity Scenario

One evening, Jasmine visits Awaken Delights’ website. She’s greeted by a clean homepage featuring product categories. She clicks on “Pastries & Treats,” browses seasonal muffins, and adds a dozen to her cart. She then selects a pick-up date and enters a special message for the label. She completes the checkout quickly and receives an email confirmation.

3. Information Design Scenario

The homepage showcases rotating banners for promotions, followed by clearly labeled sections: “Shop by Category,” “Workshops,” “Order Custom Cakes,” and “Join Our Mailing List.” The navigation menu is always visible, and the footer includes contact info, FAQs, and a “Track My Order” tool. Product pages feature brief, enticing descriptions, pricing, and high-res images.

4. Community Engagement Scenario: Nina’s Saturday Baking Bonding

Nina, a retired librarian in her early 60s, loves spending quality time with her granddaughter on weekends. She’s looking for creative, screen-free activities they can enjoy together. While browsing community event boards, she finds Awaken Delights is hosting a “Grandparent & Me Baking Workshop” and heads to the website.

Once on the site, Nina easily navigates to the “Workshops & Classes” section. The cheerful event banner and detailed class description reassure her it’s beginner-friendly. She clicks “Register Now,” selects a date, and fills out her and her granddaughter’s info in the pop-up form. After registering, she receives a confirmation email with class prep instructions, parking details, and a reminder 48 hours before the event.

That Saturday, they arrive to a warm, welcoming bakery kitchen filled with other families. They learn to make mini strawberry tarts and even get to take home recipe cards. Nina feels deeply connected to the bakery and signs up for their newsletter before leaving.

Interaction Design Scenario

The Awaken Delights site will need an intuitive UI that supports:

  • Sticky navigation bar with hover effects and dropdown menus.
  • Search tool with auto-suggestions.
  • Expandable product cards for item customization.
  • Simple and quick checkout process with scheduling options.
  • Mobile responsiveness and large, readable buttons.

10 Cognitive Psychology Principles Applied

1. Users dislike having to think and work hard to get tasks done.

Context: Simple, direct navigation and large CTA buttons will minimize cognitive load.

Relevance: Critical for time-strapped parents or event planners.

Techniques: Use icons, clear labels, and streamlined checkout.

2. Users have limitations.

Context: Not all users are tech-savvy.

Relevance: Important for older users like grandparents signing up for workshops.

Techniques: Use plain language, tooltips, and minimal required fields.

3. Users make mistakes.

Context: Users may enter wrong dates or forget to specify quantity.

Relevance: Mistakes can disrupt service and reduce trust.

Techniques: Provide error validation, undo options, and confirmations.

4. Users have imperfect memories.

Context: Users won’t remember all ingredients or past orders.

Relevance: Useful for frequent customers and newsletter subscribers.

Techniques: Save favorites, autofill past info, reminder emails.

5. Users are social animals.

Context: They value community events and shared experiences.

Relevance: Strong pull for local audiences.

Techniques: Feature user stories, reviews, and social event banners.

6. Users have selective attention.

Context: They ignore clutter.

Relevance: Prioritize high-value content.

Techniques: Use visual hierarchy and contrasting call-to-action buttons.

7. Users crave information.

Context: Want to know ingredients, preparation, pickup policies.

Relevance: Reassures and builds trust.

Techniques: Offer FAQs, blogs, and tooltips.

8. Users are driven by their unconscious mind.

Context: Emotional design matters.

Relevance: Aesthetic appeal influences perceived quality.

Techniques: Use soft colors, cozy imagery, friendly microcopy.

9. Users rely on mental models.

Context: Expect standard e-commerce patterns.

Relevance: Avoid confusing them with unconventional layouts.

Techniques: Align with familiar navigation and shopping flows.

10. Users have imperfect vision.

Context: Accessibility is key.

Relevance: Especially for older visitors.

Techniques: High contrast, scalable fonts, alt text on all images.
